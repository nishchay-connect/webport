## Overview

AMI (Artificial Medical Intelligence) is an exploration of how software and AI might make the **first contact with healthcare** less fragmented. The central interest is not replacing clinical judgment: it is improving the collection of initial context and helping people reach appropriate human care with more useful information in hand.

## The problem

A person experiencing symptoms often begins with incomplete descriptions, scattered information, and uncertainty about the next step. Context can be lost when they move between self-search, informal advice, and a medical professional.

**Working question:** Could a carefully constrained, conversational interface make that transition clearer while respecting the limits of automated assessment?

## Direction & proposed architecture

The intended workflow is a sequence rather than a single diagnosis screen:

1. **Preliminary conversation** — capture symptoms and relevant context in understandable language.
2. **Structured triage support** — organize risk signals and determine when escalation is appropriate, subject to appropriate clinical validation.
3. **Human escalation** — preserve useful context for a qualified clinician instead of starting over.

This is a **design direction**, not a claim of clinical performance or medical approval. Any real-world deployment would require medical oversight, privacy safeguards, and validation.
